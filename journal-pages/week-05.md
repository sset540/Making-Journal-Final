## Week 05
There was no class this week as Friday 03 April was a public holiday for Good Friday. In place of our usual tutorial, the week was set aside for independent study to be completed during and after the mid-semester break. The focus was on looking back across all four experiments from the first half of the course and identifying a clear direction for the Data-Driven Visualisation project.

### Reflective Proposal
The main task for this week was completing the Reflective Proposal assignment, due by 23:59 on Thursday 23 April. This involved reviewing all four Making Journal entries and writing a 500-word proposal that defined a thematic focus, a future scenario, identified data sources, and outlined a vision for the final visualisation artefact.

To begin, I worked back through my journal entries from Weeks 01 to 04 and wrote notes in response to the reflection prompts provided. Looking across the experiments, a few consistent threads stood out. In Week 01, manually tracking my music listening made the data feel personal and intentional in a way that automated tracking never does. In Week 02, translating that same dataset into an interactive p5.js sketch pushed me to think structurally about how visual logic communicates meaning. Week 03 deepened this through live APIs, and Week 04 brought everything together when I worked with the MBIE rental bond dataset through AI tools and noticed how much the administrative data flattened the actual social reality of renting.

From these reflections, I identified housing and renting in Aotearoa New Zealand as my thematic focus. The proposal articulates a future scenario in which rental data trails are used algorithmically to profile tenants, connecting to ideas about datafication of everyday life and data sovereignty discussed throughout the course. The two data sources I proposed are the MBIE Tenancy Services rental bond CSV from the NZ Government Data Service, and a self-collected personal dataset tracking my own rental-related costs and decisions, echoing the manual method from Week 01.

The vision for the visualisation is an interactive p5.js piece that places both datasets in the same visual space, using contrasting visual languages to reveal the gap between institutional data and lived experience.

### Proposal Consultation
The second key task was preparing for the Proposal Consultation scheduled for Friday 24 April. This is a 10-minute one-on-one conversation with a course teacher, structured as an Interactive Oral Assessment. Rather than a rehearsed presentation, the format is an unscripted conversation where the teacher uses prompts to explore the proposal.

To prepare, I reviewed the course guides on writing the proposal and preparing for the consultation. I also prepared a single-sided A4 notes page covering my key points across the four areas of discussion: thematic focus, data sources, visualisation approach, and intended impact.

### Reflection
Although there was no class this week, the independent study period was valuable. Having unstructured time to look back across all four experiments allowed me to see patterns in my own practice that were harder to notice week to week. The shift from analogue drawing in Week 01 to live API integration in Week 03 and AI-assisted data exploration in Week 04 felt like a coherent progression in hindsight, even if it did not always feel that way in the moment.

The process of writing the Reflective Proposal also clarified my thinking considerably. Committing to a direction in writing forced me to articulate connections between my experiments and the course concepts, such as data humanism and data sovereignty, in a more precise way than I had done before. I feel confident in the direction I have chosen and am looking forward to exploring it further in the next phase of the project.
